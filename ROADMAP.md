Research done to inform the cap/price decision (2026-09-05, user-requested, high effort):
- **Average usage**: most AI-assistant users are not daily power users (only 41% of ChatGPT's MAU use it 3+×/week). The closest real comparable to this app's cost profile, Perplexity's "Deep Research" mode, caps even paying $20/mo subscribers at only **~20/month**.
- **Worst-case/power-user %**: no AI-specific published number exists (Perplexity didn't disclose one when they cut their own limits), but the general SaaS/freemium pattern is a Pareto split -- **roughly the top 20% of users drive the large majority of usage**. Design assumption: ~1-in-5 subscribers will be a genuine heavy user regularly approaching the cap; the rest use a fraction of it.
- **Iraqi market pricing (direct comparable found)**: resold/shared ChatGPT Plus seats are actively sold on Iraqi Instagram right now for **7,000 IQD/month** -- same product category, same social-media-resale channel the user described from personal experience. Combined with Telegram Premium (~$5 ≈ 6,550 IQD) and the earlier Spotify anchor (4,900 IQD), the accepted range for a paid digital subscription in Iraq clusters around **5,000-7,000 IQD/month**. Official ChatGPT Plus ($20 ≈ 26,000 IQD) is far outside what the local market actually pays directly.
- **Cap options costed out and the final decision**: 100 (old flat cap) = 23,000 IQD worst-case, badly underwater vs. a 6-7k price; **40/30/20 (chosen) = 9,200 / ~8,910 / 6,400 IQD worst-case vs. 6,000 / 7,000 / 8,500 IQD prices -- normal and pro can lose money at full use (accepted risk), max stays profitable even then**; 25/20/15 (the more conservative option originally recommended) would have kept every tier profitable even at full use but was passed over for a more generous/appealing number.

- [x] Moderation wrongly rejecting a normal follow-up question — found live: "give me more information and sources" (a completely normal way to continue an existing research thread) was rejected as "not a specific research question." Same root cause and same fix pattern as the earlier paper-caption bug: the general moderation prompt judges text as a *freestanding* question, an unfair bar for a follow-up that only makes sense in context of an existing thread. Fixed with `moderation.format_followup_moderation_prompt()`, wired into `handle_followup_input()`. ## Known limitations (memo, 2026-09-07, not action items -- accepted tradeoffs)

Researched common complaints about other AI research tools (Perplexity, Elicit, Consensus) to see which apply here before real users arrive. Two genuinely do; the rest were checked and confirmed NOT problems this app has.

- **General Q&A mode has no structural grounding guarantee, unlike the research pipeline.** `general_qa.py`'s answer relies on a prompt instruction ("don't invent fake studies") rather than the JSON-schema/paper_id validation the research pipeline enforces -- the same failure class as the hallucinated-citation problem that hit NeurIPS/ICLR papers in 2026 (100+ confirmed fake citations found by automated scanning). Already partially mitigated: general answers are labeled in the UI as "not sourced research," so a user isn't misled into thinking it's grounded. **Decided NOT to fix further** -- doing so would mean grounding general Q&A in real search too, which defeats its purpose as the lightweight, no-search path. Accepted as a known, labeled, low-stakes residual risk.
- [x] **Extraction accuracy verification -- built 2026-09-10.** Was: no automated check that an extracted finding actually matches what its source abstract says (the subtler "cited correctly but misrepresented" failure mode, distinct from outright fabrication) -- an industry-wide unsolved problem, not unique to this app. Added a second, independent Haiku call after each paper's extraction, comparing the finding against the abstract (`synthesis.FINDING_VERIFICATION_JSON_SCHEMA`, `run_assistant.verify_finding()`, `pipeline_runner.format_finding_verification_prompt()`). Deliberately **fail-open, not a gate**: a flagged finding is logged but the search still completes normally -- a second model call is itself not ground truth, and failing a whole working search over one flagged paper would trade a subtle quality risk for a more disruptive one (same philosophy as moderation elsewhere). Threaded through `extract_one_paper()`/`run_pipeline()`/`expand_selection()`/`research_followup()`, optional and backward compatible. Tested locally with mocked callables (accurate/flagged/verifier-error/no-verifier/extraction-still-enforced), 5/5 passing, zero real API cost. Real cost impact: ~2-3% added per search (one small Haiku call per selected paper). See commit `61485a0`. **Live-verified 2026-09-10** -- ran a real search, confirmed via Streamlit Cloud logs that the verification check actually fires. Keep an eye on how often `[server-only log] Finding verification flagged...` shows up over time -- that frequency is the real signal for whether this should eventually become a harder gate instead of just a logged flag.

Confirmed NOT problems here: free-tier bait-and-switch (Perplexity cut deep searches from 600/day to 20/month; this app's 5/day free tier is stable and transparently disclosed), the well-documented Arabic-language AI gap (this app's core positioning already addresses it), weak PDF analysis (competitors are criticized for lacking this; this app has native PDF support), and a steep learning curve (this app is chat-based, the easier end of that tradeoff vs. workflow-style tools like Elicit).
